Developer Notebook

What GitHub Free actually allows

There is no hard total-repository ceiling on GitHub Free, but the platform publishes recommended limits. The number to remember is 10 GB for the on-disk .git folder. Practical guidance is tighter: many teams treat 1 GB as healthy and 5 GB as the upper bound before clone times and routine operations start to drag.

GitHub's own documentation does not enforce a hard cap on total repository size, but it does enforce a 100 MB per-file hard limit and a 2 GB single-push cap. Files larger than 100 MB must be tracked with Git LFS, which has its own separate storage and bandwidth quotas (1 GB storage and 1 GB bandwidth per month on Free). Repositories that grow large enough to strain infrastructure may receive a notice from GitHub Support asking for a size reduction, and clone times degrade noticeably beyond roughly 5 GB of history.

Market Wire

Nvidia's leadership shock ripples through global tech

The dominant story of the day is the sudden resignation of Jensen Huang as Nvidia's president, CEO, and board member, effective immediately, disclosed in SEC filings dated November 11. CFO Colette Kress has stepped in as interim CEO. Reports link the departure to revelations of extramarital affairs, one involving a woman suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset. Nvidia's board is not considering reinstatement and is focused on a national security investigation. Former President Trump publicly backed Huang's return; Treasury Secretary Bessent called for a "dramatic" leadership reshuffle.

Nvidia has lost roughly $450 billion in market value since the disclosure. Its market capitalization has fallen below $4 trillion, now down nearly 35% from its peak. The company's board has reportedly engaged outside counsel and briefed the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS) about the alleged intelligence connection. Interim CEO Kress addressed employees in an internal memo, urging focus on the upcoming earnings report and the Vera platform ramp.

Policy Desk

Washington widens the chip perimeter

The US Commerce Department issued interim rules requiring data center operators in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, and Indonesia to obtain licenses before purchasing advanced AI chips from Nvidia and other US manufacturers. The stated aim is to stop Chinese firms such as Alibaba and ByteDance from remotely accessing restricted chips through regional data centers.

The rules cover chips above a specified performance threshold, including Nvidia's H100/H200 and Blackwell-class accelerators, as well as equivalent AMD and Intel parts. Compliance obligations fall on the data center operator, not the chipmaker, and licenses are reviewed case-by-case with a presumption of denial for entities linked to restricted parties. The interim rules take effect in 60 days, with a comment period open until mid-January 2027.

Singapore Malaysia Thailand Indonesia License required Advanced AI chips 60-day window

The measure deepens the US–China technology split and landed on an already fragile market, adding to the selling pressure in semiconductor names. Regional cloud providers in the four countries could see delays of several months on new GPU capacity, and some operators are reportedly exploring workarounds via third-country leasing arrangements.

Hardware & Silicon

Four moves that matter in AI infrastructure

Nvidia Vera CPU rack-scale platform
  • 88 custom Olympus cores and 176 threads per CPU.
  • SOCAMM2 LPDDR5X memory delivering up to 1.2 TB/s bandwidth.
  • A single rack: 192 Vera CPUs, roughly 300 TB of memory, and about 1 PB of storage, linked by Quantum-X800 InfiniBand.
  • Nvidia claims 1.9× the performance and 2.4× performance per watt versus x86 on the OpenFOAM motorBike simulation test.
  • Each Vera CPU uses a PCIe Gen 6 host interface and supports CXL 3.1 for memory pooling.
  • The German Climate Computing Center (DKRZ) has selected Vera for its HLRE-5 supercomputer.
  • Racks available from Dell, HPE, Lenovo, and Supermicro starting early 2027.
Google Cloud Ironwood TPU benchmarks

In MLPerf Training v6.1, Google Cloud reported that 8,192 Ironwood TPUs completed the DeepSeek-V3 pre-training task in 1.70 minutes (about 102 seconds), with 96% scaling efficiency when expanded to 4,096 chips. In the first agentic reinforcement-learning benchmark, 640 Ironwood TPUs reached 76.5% accuracy on a coding task (target 69%) in 18.02 minutes, with training-chip idle time under 3%.

Samsung builds custom HBM for Meta

Samsung is developing custom High Bandwidth Memory for Meta's self-developed AI chip, based on HBM4E, with the base die manufactured on Samsung Foundry's 2nm process. The deal expands Samsung's custom HBM customer base beyond Nvidia and AMD to another major technology company designing its own silicon.

The custom HBM is expected to enter risk production in H2 2027, with volume ramp in 2028. Samsung is positioning the 2nm base die as a differentiator against SK Hynix and Micron, which currently lead the HBM market. Meta's chip program, reportedly codenamed "Artemis," is intended for inference workloads across its data centers.

AMD completes World Labs acquisition; Fei-Fei Li joins

AMD closed its acquisition of World Labs, the AI research company founded by Fei-Fei Li that focuses on interactive 3D environments and robot learning and simulation. Fei-Fei Li joins AMD as Executive Vice President and Chief Scientist, reporting directly to CEO Lisa Su. The World Labs team will continue foundational model research and work with AMD engineers on future AI infrastructure.

Financial terms were not disclosed. The deal is AMD's largest AI talent acquisition to date and signals a push beyond chip design into spatial intelligence and simulation workloads that feed robotics and autonomous systems.

Short Takes

Elsewhere on the wire

Tesla Berlin expansion

Tesla plans to invest €3 billion to expand its Gigafactory in Berlin. Production is expected to begin in 2029, with a long-term goal of building up to 1 million Cybercab vehicles per year.

The expansion includes new battery cell lines and a dedicated Cybercab assembly hall. Local approvals are still pending, and environmental groups have signaled they will challenge the water-use permits.

Baidu chip progress

Baidu's Kunlunxin M100 chip is expected to be ready for mass production by the end of the year, as the company's AI cloud infrastructure revenue continues to grow strongly.

The M100 is aimed at inference workloads and is fabricated on a domestic Chinese process node. Baidu says it has already deployed early units in its own data centers and plans to offer them to external customers in 2027.

California sues OpenAI

The California Attorney General filed a lawsuit against OpenAI, alleging the company developed advanced AI models without adequate safety precautions and monitoring, endangering public safety. [From earlier summary — no corroborating current coverage]

The complaint reportedly cites internal documents and whistleblower accounts, and seeks injunctive relief plus civil penalties. OpenAI has not issued a public response.
